{\Huge \textbf{Notation}}

\vspace{0.6cm}

A recurring difficulty in a text of this kind is that probabilists and practitioners
often write the same object in different ways, and occasionally write different
objects in the same way. The following conventions are fixed once and used
without exception throughout. Where a financial convention differs from the
probabilistic one, the discrepancy is pointed out in a footnote at the place where
it first matters.

\vspace{0.4cm}

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.25}
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.20\textwidth}p{0.74\textwidth}@{}}
\hline
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\textbf{Probability space and information}}\\
\hline
$\PSpace$ & Complete probability space; $\PP$ is always the \emph{physical} measure.\\
$\QQ$ & Risk-neutral measure associated with the numeraire $\Num$.\\
$\QT$ & $T$-forward measure, associated with the numeraire $\Bond{\cdot}{T}$.\\
$\Filt$ & Filtration, always satisfying the usual conditions (Definition~\ref{def:usualconditions}).\\
$\FiltW$ & Augmented filtration generated by the Brownian motion $\Wt$.\\
$\E[\,\cdot\,]$, $\Ep{\QQ}[\,\cdot\,]$ & Expectation under $\PP$, and under a named measure.\\
$\Var[\,\cdot\,]$ & Variance. (Not to be confused with $V$, an option value.)\\
$\Borel(E)$ & Borel $\sigma$-algebra of the topological space $E$.\\
$\Ind_A$ & Indicator function of the event $A$.\\
\hline
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\textbf{Time}}\\
\hline
$[0,T]$ & Time interval. Always anchored at $0$; $T$ is simultaneously the time horizon and the maturity of the contract under consideration, which is exactly why the same letter is used.\footnotemark\\
$\tau$ & A stopping time; $\Stop{[t,T]}$ is the set of stopping times valued in $[t,T]$.\\
\hline
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\textbf{Processes}}\\
\hline
$\Wt$ & Brownian motion. The letter $B$ is never used for it.\\
$\Wvec$ & Multidimensional Brownian motion with independent components.\\
$\BH$ & Fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter $H$.\\
$\mu$ & A \emph{drift}, always. Abstract measures are denoted $\nu$, $\lambda$.\\
$\sigma$ & A \emph{diffusion coefficient}; $\Sigma=\sigma\sigma\transp$ is the diffusion matrix.\\
$\rho$ & A \emph{correlation}, always. Densities are denoted $p$ or $f$.\\
$p_{s,x}(t,y)$ & Transition density of a Markov process.\\
$\rqv{X}$, $\qv{X}$ & Realised (pathwise) and predictable quadratic variation of $X$; the two coincide for the continuous processes considered here.\\
$\Gen$, $\Genadj$ & Infinitesimal generator of a Markov semigroup and its adjoint.\\
$\Expo{\alpha}$ & Stochastic (Doléans-Dade) exponential of $\int\alpha\,\dd \Wt$.\\
\hline
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\textbf{Finance}}\\
\hline
$\Spot_t$ & Spot price of the underlying.\\
$\Fwd_t$ or $\Fwd(t,T)$ & Forward price for delivery at $T$.\\
$\Bond{t}{T}$ & Price at $t$ of a zero-coupon bond maturing at $T$.\\
$\Num$ & Numeraire asset.\\
$\Payoff_T$, $\PayoffFn$ & Terminal payoff (a random variable) and payoff function.\\
$\Strike$ & Strike price; $\lmon=\log(\Strike/\Fwd)$ is log-moneyness.\\
$\rf$, $\divy$ & Short rate and continuous dividend (or repo) yield.\\
$\ivol$, $\lvol$ & Implied and local volatility; $\tvar=\ivol^2\,T$ is total implied variance.\\
$V$ & The value of a derivative. (Not to be confused with $\Var$, variance.)\\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\footnotetext{Probability texts customarily work on a generic interval $[a,b]$.
We deliberately fix $a=0$: in a financial context the present instant is the natural
origin, every quoted quantity is a function of time-to-maturity, and carrying an
arbitrary left endpoint through the pricing formulas obscures rather than
generalises. Nothing is lost, since any statement on $[a,b]$ transfers by
translation.}
