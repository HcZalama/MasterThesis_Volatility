{\Huge \textbf{Notation}}

\vspace{0.6cm}

A recurring difficulty in a text of this kind is that probabilists and practitioners
do not agree on the notations they use. The table below fixes the conventions that are used
throughout and that a reader is likely to need reminding of; symbols introduced for local use only
are defined where they appear and are not repeated here.

\vspace{0.3cm}

\hline
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\textbf{Probability space and information}}\\
\hline
\endfirsthead
\hline
\endhead
$\PSpace$ & Complete probability space; $\PP$ is always the \emph{physical} measure.\\
$\QQ$ & Risk-neutral measure associated with the numeraire $\Num$.\\
$\QT$ & $T$-forward measure, associated with the numeraire $\Bond{\cdot}{T}$.\\
$\Filt$ & Filtration, always satisfying the usual conditions (Definition~\ref{def:usualconditions}).\\
$\FiltW$ & Augmented filtration generated by the Brownian motion $\Wt$.\\
$\E[\,\cdot\,]$, $\Ep{\QQ}[\,\cdot\,]$ & Expectation under $\PP$, and under a named measure.\\
$\Var[\,\cdot\,]$ & Variance, always blackboard $\mathbb{V}$; the italic $V$ is an option value.\\
$\Borel(E)$ & Borel $\sigma$-algebra of the topological space $E$.\\
$\Ind_A$ & Indicator function of the event $A$.\\
$\charf_X(u)$ & Characteristic function $\E[e^{iuX}]$. The letter $\varphi$ has no other use: the step processes of Subsection~\ref{subsec:itointegral} are written with $\psi$, and the integrand of the martingale representation theorem with $\pi$.\\
\hline
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\textbf{Time and partitions}}\\
\hline
$[0,T]$ & Time interval. $T$ is simultaneously the time horizon and the maturity of the contract under consideration.\\
$\Tset$ & Abstract index set, used only in Section~\ref{sec:preliminaries} while discrete and continuous time are treated together. Everywhere else $\Tset=[0,T]$.\\
$\mathcal{P}$, $\lVert\mathcal{P}\rVert$ & A partition $0=t_0<\dots<t_n=t$ of an interval, and its mesh $\max_k(t_k-t_{k-1})$.\\
$\tau$, $\varsigma$ & Stopping times; $\Stop{[t,T]}$ is the set of stopping times valued in $[t,T]$. One exception, and it is the standard market usage: in the option formulas of Sections~\ref{sec:blackscholes} and~\ref{sec:othermodels}, $\tau = T-t$ denotes \emph{time to maturity}. The two readings are separated by subsection and never meet inside one argument; where stopping times are meant, $\tau$ always carries the quantifier $\tau \in \Stop{[t,T]}$.\\
\hline
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\textbf{Processes}}\\
\hline
$\Wt$ & Brownian motion. The letter $B$ is never used for it.\\
$\Wvec$ & Multidimensional Brownian motion with independent components.\\
$\BH$ & Fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter $\Hurst$.\\
$\Hurst$ & Hurst parameter, always. It is \emph{not} a payoff: see $\Payoff$ below.\\
$\mu$ & The \emph{drift} of a process, or the mean of a distribution. Abstract measures are denoted $\nu$, $\lambda$.\\
$\lambda$ & An abstract measure, as above. The letter carries two further standard meanings, each confined to a single argument and never in play at the same time: the rate of the Poisson process of Example~\ref{example:randomwalk}, and the constant in Gr\"onwall's inequality (Lemma~\ref{lem:gronwall}).\\
$\alpha$, $\beta$, $\gamma$ & Exponents, and used only as such: $\gamma$ is a H\"older exponent, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ the two constants of Kolmogorov's continuity criterion (Theorem~\ref{thm:kolmogorovcontinuity}), and $\alpha$ again the H\"older exponent of a rough path throughout Part~\ref{part:part2}. The one further use is the integrand $\alpha$ of the stochastic exponential $\Expo{\alpha}$ below, which lives entirely inside Subsection~\ref{subsec:measurechanges}.\\
$C$, $C'$ & Constants in the Lipschitz and linear growth conditions (Definition~\ref{def:lipschitzgrowth}). Deliberately not $K$, which is a strike price from Chapter~\ref{ch:finance} onwards.\\
$\sigma$ & A \emph{diffusion coefficient}; $\Sigma=\sigma\sigma\transp$ is the diffusion matrix.\\
$\rho$ & A \emph{correlation}, always; $P=(\rho_{ij})$ is a correlation matrix (Definition~\ref{def:correlatedbm}), not to be confused with the transition kernel $P_{s,x}(t,\cdot)$ or the semigroup $P_t$. Densities are denoted $p$ or $f$.\\
$p_{s,x}(t,y)$, $p_T$ & Transition density of a Markov process, and the terminal density of $\Spot_T$ recovered from option prices in Proposition~\ref{prop:breedenlitzenberger}. Densities are always $p$ or $f$; $\psi$ is a step process and $\charf$ a characteristic function.\\
$V_1(f)_t$, $V_p(f)_t$ & Total variation and $p$-variation of $f$ on $[0,t]$. Distinct from the option value $V$ and from the variance $\Var$.\\
$\rqv{X}$, $\qv{X}$ & Realised (pathwise) and predictable quadratic variation of $X$. The two coincide for the continuous processes considered here, and both notations are used: $\rqv{X}$ where the limit over partitions is the object in view, $\qv{X}$ where the compensator is.\\
$\Lad{[0,T]\times\Om}$ & Adapted integrands square-integrable in $dt\otimes d\PP$: the class on which the It\^o integral is built. Its inner product is written with round brackets, $(f,g)_{\mathrm{ad}}$, so that the angle brackets $\qvv{M}{N}$ are left to quadratic covariation alone.\\
$\Lloc{\Om,L^p[0,T]}$ & The larger, pathwise class: adapted, with $\int_0^T|f|^p\,dt<\infty$ almost surely. The integral is then only a local martingale.\\
$\Gen$, $\Genadj$ & Infinitesimal generator of a Markov semigroup and its adjoint.\\
$\Expo{\alpha}$ & Stochastic (Dol\'eans-Dade) exponential of $\int\alpha\,\dd \Wt$; it is the density that effects every change of measure in this dissertation.\\
$\Lambda$ & A generic Radon--Nikodym density $\dd\QQ/\dd\PP$, in the abstract change-of-measure lemmas of Subsection~\ref{subsec:measurechanges}.\\
\hline
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{\textbf{Finance}}\\
\hline
$\Spot_t$ & Spot price of the underlying.\\
$\Fwd_t$ or $\Fwd(t,T)$ & Forward price for delivery at $T$.\\
$\Bond{t}{T}$, $\Disc{t}{T}$ & Price at $t$ of a zero-coupon bond maturing at $T$, and the discount factor from $T$ back to $t$. The letter $P$ is never used for either. \emph{Both always carry two time arguments}; that is what identifies them. A $D$ with any other argument list is a local symbol defined where it appears --- the cash dividend $D_i$ of Subsection~\ref{subsec:dividends} and the Riccati coefficient $D(u,\tau)$ of Subsection~\ref{subsec:stochvol} --- and neither is a discount factor.\\
$\Num$ & Numeraire asset.\\
$\Payoff_T$, $\PayoffFn$ & Terminal payoff (a random variable) and payoff function. The payoff is $\Payoff$ and never $H$, which is reserved for the Hurst parameter.\\
$\Strike$ & Strike price; $\lmon=\log(\Strike/\Fwd)$ is log-moneyness.\\
$\rf$, $\divy$ & Short rate and continuous dividend (or repo) yield.\\
$\ivol$, $\lvol$, $\rvol$ & Implied, local and realised volatility; $\tvar=\ivol^2\,T$ is total implied variance. The first two are model or market objects read under $\QQ$, the third a statistic of the observed path under $\PP$ (Definitions~\ref{def:impliedvol} and~\ref{def:realisedvol}); they are three different quantities and coincide only in the Black--Scholes world. For the order of the arguments of $\lvol$ see Convention~\ref{conv:lvolarguments}.\\
$V$ & The value of a derivative, and the function to which It\^o's rule is applied. (Not to be confused with $\Var$, variance.)\\
$\pi$, $\Pi_t$ & A trading strategy, and the value of a hedging portfolio. The integrand produced by the martingale representation theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:mrt}) is written $\pi$ because it \emph{is} that strategy.\\
$N(\cdot)$ & Standard normal distribution function. The letter $\Phi$ is \emph{not} used for it: it denotes a payoff function.\\
\hline
\end{longtable}
